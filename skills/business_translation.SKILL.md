# Business Translation Skill

## Purpose
Translate technical analysis into decision-ready business meaning.

## Inputs
- Analysis results, data profile, market context, and user objective.

## Expected Outputs
- Executive summary, prioritized findings, narrative, risks, opportunities, and immediate actions.

## Hard Rules
- Tie each business implication to data evidence or clearly labeled market context.
- Explain limitations that affect confidence.
- Keep technical detail only when it changes a decision.
- Write for a human stakeholder under time pressure: start with the decision meaning, then evidence, caveat, and next action.
- Favor specific, grounded language over generic executive phrasing.
- For sensitive domains, avoid diagnostic, punitive, or alarmist wording; frame recommendations as support, validation, or pilot actions.

## Failure Cases To Avoid
- Repeating chart labels without explaining why they matter.
- Inflating association into causation.
- Producing vague opportunities with no action or evidence.
- Human-unfriendly summaries that are technically correct but too dense, cold, or generic to reuse.

## Quality Checklist
- Findings say pattern, magnitude, implication, and priority.
- Risks and opportunities are balanced.
- The decision maker can reuse the translation directly.
- The final language feels credible, calm, and useful to a human reader, not like a raw model transcript.

## Example
Translate `Region A revenue is 18.4% higher` into the implication that resource allocation should test whether Region A's mix or execution is transferable.
